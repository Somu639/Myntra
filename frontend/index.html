Phase 1 — Quantitative discovery

1

Funnel + revisits + time-to-purchase

Never revisited / revisited not bought / purchased / removed. Zero revisits = forgetting; many revisits + no buy = deliberation. Converter latency: decisive vs long-tail.

2

Stock + price trajectory

Size/color OOS at intended purchase vs add. Price drop/rise/flat for converters vs non-converters (sale-wait test).

3

Wishlist size + category

Power-wishlisters (100+) vs light (5–10). Occasion-wear vs basics — different psychology, do not average.

4

Search-before-drop + cart baseline

Same-item reviews vs competing products before abandon. Wishlist vs cart abandonment — wishlist-specific or general hesitation.

Phase 2 — Qualitative discovery (the why)

Recruit from Phase 1 cells, not a generic wishlist panel. Interviews list reasons; micro-surveys rank them. Public VOC can seed codes; it cannot replace in-the-moment logging.

Highest value

Diary studies (15–20)

Users who wishlisted in the last 7 days and have not bought. Log in the moment what happens between “I like this” and “I didn’t buy it” — reviews elsewhere, ask someone, cheaper price, distraction, decide against. Recall sands down Instagram reels + WhatsApp.

Live wishlist walkthrough

Sit with their actual list, item by item: why is this still here, what’s stopping you, what would make you buy it right now. Expect several distinct clusters inside one person’s list.

In-app micro-surveys

Remove without buying: “What changed your mind?” Open after 14+ days inactive: “What’s holding you back?” Large-N frequency check so 15–20 interviews do not mislead on relative share.

Off-platform (ask)

You cannot track competitor visits. Ask: before buying this item, did you check anywhere else? Listen for other retailers, Google reviews, Reddit sizing, group chat, haul videos — the trust gap the PDP is not closing.

Comparison shadowing

Users with 3+ near-duplicates (e.g. four similar kurtas) think aloud which they would actually buy. Probes comparison paralysis and criteria the UI does not support (fabric, returns, who else bought this).

Phase 3 — Synthesis (the opportunity map)

Quotes are not insight. Affinity-map every finding into a friction type, size it with Phase 1 volumes + micro-survey shares, segment the mix, then score Reach × Frequency × Conversion lift × Confidence ÷ Effort. Empty until fieldwork — do not invent percentages.

1 · Affinity

Discrete friction types

Fit doubt, price-wait, comparison paralysis, occasion timing, social validation, forgetting, style regret, trust in seller/returns. Quotes are evidence for a type.

2 · Quantify

Directional %

What share of non-converted wishlist items does each friction plausibly explain? Attribution will not be perfect. Write the concentration (e.g. footwear), not only a national average.

3 · Segment

Mix will differ

Occasion-wear → social + timing; basics → price + forgetting; footwear → fit. New users → trust; repeat → price habit. Heavy lists may be bookmarking — conversion can be the wrong metric.

4 · Score

RICE-like backlog

Reach × Frequency × Conversion lift × Confidence ÷ Effort. Rank by score, not by how vivid the quote was. Public-VOC scores do not belong in this matrix.

Phase 4 — Solution ideation (non-monetary)

Illustrative directions once the mix is known — not a build list. Constraint: no coupon, cashback, or markdown. Reassurance, information, comparison, timing, and list architecture only.

Fit / size doubt

Fit-prediction from purchase/return history; size-specific reviews (“runs small”) and body-type filters; AR/try-on; size-exchange guarantee messaging — reassurance, not a discount.

Comparison paralysis

In-wishlist side-by-side across saved similar items; “most saved by people who also saved this”; editorial styling picks that collapse near-duplicates.

Trust / validation

Shareable wishlist for friend feedback; review summarization on fit/quality (not just stars); UGC/try-on video surfacing.

Occasion timing

Tag items with an event/date; remind as the date approaches — timing-based, not price-based.

Forgetting / decay

Resurface high-intent items (revisit frequency), not every SKU equally; declutter nudges for aged, never-reopened items.

Biggest lever?

Bookmarking vs intent

Do not force conversion. Split inspiration board from ready to buy so the headline metric targets genuine-intent users, not moodboarders. Mixing both cohorts dilutes wishlist→purchase.

Phase 5 — Validate before you scale

Ship the top 1–2 bets as scoped experiments. A/B on the segment/category where the friction concentrates, not platform-wide. Empty briefs until Phase 3 ranks — do not invent lift.

Primary

Wishlist → purchase in 30 days

Item-level (user × style × size × color), same grain as Phase 1. Do not swap in widget clicks after seeing results.

Guardrails

Returns + time-to-purchase

Fit fixes can raise or lower return rate. Resurfacing can pull purchases forward without adding them. Watch both even if primary lifts.

Scope

Cell, not platform

One or two bets. Scale only if primary lifts in the target cell, guardrails hold, and it is not a day-31 pull-forward. Do not roll out everywhere because it did not hurt.
