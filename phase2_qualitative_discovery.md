# Phase 2 — Qualitative discovery (the “why”)

Phase 1 tells you **where** drop-off concentrates. Only talking to people tells you **why**. Mix methods so one bias (recall, small-n, survey wording) does not dominate.

**Recruit from Phase 1 cells** (category × price band × tenure × wishlist-size), not from a generic “wishlist users” panel.

**North-star:** still wishlist / consideration → purchase conversion on Myntra.

Public VOC (`collect` → `extract`) can seed reason codes. It is **not** a substitute for in-the-moment diaries or live wishlist walkthroughs.

---

## Output

A **reason inventory** (what reasons exist) plus a **frequency check** (which reasons are common):

- Clusters of item-level “why this is still here / what would make me buy it now”
- Off-platform information gaps the PDP is not closing
- Decision criteria used when comparing near-duplicates
- Large-N micro-survey shares to keep the 15–20 interview sample honest on relative frequency

---

## Methods (mix on purpose)

### 1. Contextual / diary studies (highest value)

**Who:** 15–20 users who wishlisted an item in the **last 7 days** and have not bought it. Prefer densest Phase 1 cells.

**What:** they log **in the moment**, not recalled later, what happens between “I like this” and “I didn’t buy it”:

- check reviews elsewhere
- ask someone
- hunt a cheaper price
- get distracted
- decide against it entirely

Recall-only interviews sand down messy real behavior (three Instagram reels + a friend’s WhatsApp). Near-real-time logging is the point.

### 2. Task-based usability interviews (live wishlist)

Sit with users on **their actual wishlist**. Item by item:

- Why is this still here?
- What’s stopping you?
- What would make you buy it **right now**?

This is item-level, not aggregate. Expect several distinct clusters **inside one person’s list**.

### 3. In-app micro-surveys (large-N, low-cost)

Single question, contextual, non-intrusive. Validates whether interview reasons are *common*, not just *real*.

| Trigger | Question | Example codes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Remove from wishlist without purchase | What changed your mind? | found cheaper elsewhere / not sure about fit / changed my mind on style / no longer needed / other |
| Open a wishlist item after 14+ days inactive | What’s holding you back on this one? | (same codebook + “forgot / no occasion yet”) |

15–20 interviews can mislead on **relative frequency** even when they are excellent at surfacing **what reasons exist**. These surveys are the frequency check.

### 4. Off-platform behavior (ask; you cannot track competitor visits)

In interviews and a short survey:

> Before buying [wishlisted item], did you check anywhere else? Where? What did you look for there?

Listen for:

- same product on another retailer (price)
- Google “[brand] [product] reviews”
- sizing on a forum / Reddit
- friend group chat
- YouTube / Instagram try-on haul

That is the **trust or information gap** the PDP is not closing. Public VOC already flags off-platform channels; Phase 2 is the prompted, item-specific version.

### 5. Comparison-shopping shadowing

For users with **3+ near-duplicate** wishlisted items (e.g. four similar kurtas): think-aloud while choosing which they would actually buy.

Directly probes **comparison paralysis** and surfaces decision criteria the UI does not support today (fabric, return policy, social proof of “who else bought this”).

---

## How this sits next to Phase 1 and public VOC

| Source | Strength | Weakness |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phase 1 funnel | Where, at scale | Silent on motive |
| Diaries + live wishlist | Messy real *why*, item-level | Small n; frequency unreliable |
| Micro-surveys | Frequency at high-signal moments | Shallow; codes must come from qualitative first |
| Public VOC | Unprompted complaints, cheap | Not a wishlist funnel; biased to loud post-purchase pain |

Do not skip Phase 1 and recruit “anyone with a wishlist.” Do not skip micro-surveys and treat 18 interview quotes as a ranking. Ranking and sizing those reasons is [Phase 3](phase3_synthesis.md).
